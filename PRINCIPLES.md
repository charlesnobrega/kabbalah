# Kabbalah Principles

These principles define the project more strongly than any specific framework, model, provider, or implementation technique.

## User sovereignty

The user is the root authority of a Kabbalah installation.

Models, agents, tools, memories, learned strategies, federated peers, and future evolutionary mechanisms remain subordinate to that authority.

## Capability and authority are separate

A system becoming more capable does not grant it additional authority.

> Capability may evolve. Authority may not self-expand.

## HITL is guidance, not a security substitute

Human-in-the-loop interaction should help clarify intent, resolve ambiguity, correct assumptions, communicate preferences, and redirect goals.

Security must not depend on the user understanding every technical risk or repeatedly approving actions until approval becomes habitual.

## Learn, but distrust learning

Anything learned may later prove incomplete, outdated, biased, contaminated, or wrong.

Memory and learned knowledge must remain revisable.

## Evidence over inherited confidence

Confidence from a model, memory system, majority, or previous result is not the same as evidence.

Where practical, Kabbalah should preserve provenance, contradiction, confidence, observation, and correction around important knowledge.

## Controlled effects

Reasoning and execution are different responsibilities.

Intelligence may propose actions. External effects should occur through controlled, observable, and attributable execution boundaries.

## Measurable robustness

Security, containment, reliability, and resistance to contamination should be evaluated empirically rather than assumed from design claims.

A system that is safe only because it is incapable is not the target. Kabbalah aims to preserve useful autonomy while limiting unintended authority and blast radius.

## Evolution behind invariants

Learning and evolution may improve permitted capabilities, but the mechanism doing the improving must not silently redefine the authority that governs it.

## Research permeability

Kabbalah must remain able to absorb better research without requiring a new permanent subsystem for every useful paper, framework, or technique.

New research should enter when it replaces, simplifies, or measurably improves an existing capability.

## Architectural discipline

External projects are references and sources of mechanisms, not identities to be stacked inside Kabbalah.

The final architecture should describe Kabbalah concepts rather than becoming a catalog of borrowed framework names.

## Resource awareness

Intelligence is constrained by real resources: time, compute, memory, context, money, latency, and human attention.

Improvement should consider capability gained per resource consumed, not capability in isolation.

## Local sovereignty in collaboration

Shared learning must not require surrendering local control.

Federated or collaborative mechanisms should preserve privacy, provenance, local validation, and the right of an installation to reject external knowledge.
