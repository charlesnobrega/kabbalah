# Kabbalah Vision

Kabbalah is being built as a sovereign runtime for autonomous intelligence.

Its purpose is not to replace foundation models, coding agents, or research systems. Instead, Kabbalah is intended to provide a user-controlled environment in which different models and capabilities can reason, act, remember, learn, evolve, and collaborate without transferring ultimate authority away from the user.

## The problem

Modern AI systems are becoming increasingly capable, but capability and authority are often bundled together inside products, platforms, or model-specific runtimes. Memory is usually fragmented, learning is often temporary, tool access can become difficult to audit, and safety frequently depends on the user understanding technical risks well enough to approve or reject an action.

Kabbalah takes a different direction.

The system is designed around a simple thesis:

> Capability may evolve. Authority may not self-expand.

The user remains the root authority of the installation. Models, agents, memories, tools, learned strategies, and future evolutionary mechanisms remain subordinate to that authority.

## What Kabbalah should be able to do

Kabbalah is organized around six durable capabilities:

### Govern

Keep authority, policy, identity, permissions, limits, and accountability under explicit control.

### Reason

Use the most appropriate reasoning strategy, model, or combination of specialists for the task instead of binding the system to a single model or fixed orchestration pattern.

### Act

Interact with tools and external systems through controlled execution boundaries while keeping effects observable and attributable.

### Remember

Maintain useful long-term state without treating every stored observation as permanent truth.

### Learn

Improve from experience, feedback, evidence, evaluation, and failure. Learning may affect memory, skills, strategies, routing, workflows, and other permitted capabilities without automatically expanding authority.

### Share

Allow independent Kabbalah installations to exchange validated knowledge or evidence while preserving local sovereignty and privacy.

## Human guidance

Human-in-the-loop interaction is intended primarily as guidance, not as a substitute for security.

The user should be able to clarify intent, choose between legitimate alternatives, correct assumptions, teach preferences, and redirect goals. Technical safety should not depend on the user being a security expert or repeatedly approving prompts they may not fully understand.

## Learning with uncertainty

Kabbalah should not treat model confidence, stored memory, popularity, repetition, or inherited training data as equivalent to truth.

Knowledge should remain revisable. Where practical, claims should be connected to evidence, provenance, confidence, contradiction, observation, and later correction.

The system should be able to recognize that what it previously learned may be incomplete, biased, contaminated, outdated, or simply wrong.

## Evolution

Kabbalah is intended to benefit continuously from advances in AI research without turning each new technique into another permanent architectural layer.

New research should be absorbed when it demonstrably improves an existing Kabbalah capability. Techniques may change. The core principles should remain stable.

Evolution is therefore not unrestricted self-modification. It is controlled improvement under invariant authority boundaries and measurable evaluation.

## Robustness

Kabbalah treats robustness as something to measure rather than claim.

Capability improvements should be evaluated together with resilience against loss of control, contaminated learning, unintended authority expansion, resource amplification, unsafe execution, and other failure modes relevant to autonomous systems.

The long-term goal is not merely a capable agent. It is an autonomous intelligence runtime that can improve without making user sovereignty progressively weaker.

## Direction

Kabbalah remains an evolving research and engineering project. Some capabilities already exist in experimental form, others are being reworked, and others remain research directions.

The project should stay open to better ideas without losing its identity every time the field changes.
