# Contributing to Kabbalah

Kabbalah's public repository is focused on the project's vision, principles, research direction, status, and selected public results.

Detailed implementation, internal architecture, security mechanisms, private benchmarks, and development roadmaps are maintained separately.

## Useful public contributions

Public contributions are most useful when they improve one of the following:

- clarity of the project vision or principles;
- research references and comparisons;
- public documentation quality;
- conceptual critique of the architecture;
- proposals for measurable capability or robustness evaluation;
- reproducible public findings relevant to autonomous intelligence, memory, learning, evolution, human-agent collaboration, security, or distributed intelligence.

## Research proposals

A proposal should explain:

1. what problem or Kabbalah capability it affects;
2. what evidence supports the idea;
3. what existing approach it may replace, simplify, or improve;
4. expected benefits and tradeoffs;
5. whether it belongs in near-term engineering or should remain research.

Interesting technology alone is not enough reason to expand the architecture.

## Security research

Defensive security discussion is welcome, including robustness, containment, poisoning resistance, prompt-injection resilience, capability boundaries, sandboxing, and adversarial evaluation.

Please do not publish operational secrets, private implementation details, credentials, sensitive internal findings, or material that unnecessarily increases real-world abuse capability.

## Project principles

Before proposing a structural change, read:

- [Vision](VISION.md)
- [Principles](PRINCIPLES.md)
- [Research](RESEARCH.md)
- [Status](STATUS.md)

The project is intentionally open to new research while trying to avoid architectural sprawl.

## Status claims

Do not describe a capability as complete, production-ready, secure, or validated without evidence appropriate to that claim.

Prefer precise language such as:

- operational;
- evolving;
- experimental;
- research;
- partially validated.

## Discussions and issues

Use GitHub discussions or issues for public proposals, research references, conceptual questions, documentation corrections, and reproducible public findings.

Implementation-specific collaboration is handled through the private engineering repository.
